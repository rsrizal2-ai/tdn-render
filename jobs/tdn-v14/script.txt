Three tonnes of meth, moved through military airbases — and police say insiders opened the gates. This week, Malaysia's Home Minister told Parliament that a syndicate smuggled three tonnes of methamphetamine, worth one hundred fifty-six million ringgit, through Royal Malaysian Air Force bases in Tawau and Labuan. Twenty-one shipments slipped through since August twenty twenty-four. The Tawau cell alone moved one point eight tonnes. How does that much meth cross a guarded runway? Investigators say the syndicate had people with direct access to air force personnel and flight operations. Police tracked the network for months, then shared their intelligence with the air force. On November seventeen and nineteen, the air force's own military police struck first: thirteen arrests — seven air force personnel, five of them officers, plus six civilians. Another ninety-six kilos of meth were seized. The suspected mastermind is identified, but still on the run. Six suspects now face trafficking charges — charges that can carry the death penalty. Three tonnes of meth is enough for fifteen million doses. The minister's warning was blunt: no rank will protect anyone. The hunt is still on. The Daily Toon News — the world, in toons.
