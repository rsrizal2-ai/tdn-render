They flew to India for a dream job. Their passports were taken at the door.

This week, Delhi police raided a compound in Noida, on the edge of the capital, and rescued one hundred sixty-four young people: ninety-two Indonesians and seventy-two Malaysians, most of them aged nineteen to twenty-five.

Police say they were lured by promises of well-paid work. Instead, their passports were seized, their movements were restricted, and they were trained and forced to run fake investment scams, calling and messaging victims back home in Indonesia and Malaysia. Those who refused, police say, were assaulted.

Inside the compound, officers seized three hundred fifty-five mobile phones, one hundred sixty-eight computers, twenty-six laptops, fifty-three SIM cards, and one hundred seventy-four passports.

One suspect, a twenty-five-year-old Malaysian man, has been arrested, and his role is under investigation. Two alleged key members of the network are on the run, with more than ten police teams hunting them across five states.

The job offer was the trap. And police say the hunt for the people behind it is far from over.
