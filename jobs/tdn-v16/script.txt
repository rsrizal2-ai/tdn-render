A US warplane has just shot a cargo ship to a stop in the Gulf of Oman.

The ship was trying to run America's naval blockade of Iran. It never stood a chance.

On Thursday, the Pacific Archer, a merchant ship flying the flag of Palau, sailed out of the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas, bound for Singapore.

American commanders say the crew ignored repeated warnings and kept going.

So a US military aircraft fired precision munitions straight into the ship's stern. The strike knocked out its propulsion, and the huge vessel ground to a halt in open water. Its civilian crew was unharmed.

The Pacific Archer is at least the fifteenth ship disabled since the blockade began in the spring, and the sixth since it resumed in July, after a pause for peace talks. Earlier this month, Washington also unveiled Task Force Iron Gate, a new push to choke off weapons and sanctioned goods crossing Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea.

Tehran is refusing to back down. An adviser to Iran's supreme leader says the Strait of Hormuz will not be reopened, and Iran will not allow the blockade to remain in place.

Two fleets. One narrow sea. And a blockade neither side will abandon. The next ship that tries to run it now knows exactly what is waiting for it.
