Eleven days. That is how long Nvidia, the world's most valuable chip company, lasted without its founder, Jensen Huang.

On November twelfth, Nvidia announced Huang had resigned. Reports said the board had lost confidence after revelations of an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed — a woman US officials reportedly suspected of working with Chinese intelligence. No evidence of unlawful conduct has been made public.

The fall was brutal. Nvidia reportedly lost about one point two trillion dollars in market value. And on November seventeenth, the board insisted Huang would not be reinstated.

Then Washington stepped in. President Trump publicly urged Nvidia to take him back, and senior officials pressed the board directly. On November twenty-third, the board reversed itself, unanimously. Huang returned as chief executive — though not as chairman.

Three days later came the twist. Reports said Nvidia had told Dell and Supermicro to halt AI server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia, after a new US rule required export licences — a move aimed at alleged chip routes into China.

Investigations are still open. This saga is not over.
