Thirty people went out to buy groceries in Colombia. They came home seventeen hours later, freed by special forces. On Tuesday morning, an armed crew tried to rob an armored cash truck outside a supermarket in Cartagena, a Caribbean port city packed with tourists. But the truck had already left. So the gunmen ran inside, and took the whole store hostage: about thirty shoppers and workers. They demanded seven billion pesos, about two million dollars, and a way out. The windows were papered over. Police and soldiers surrounded the block. Through the day, ten hostages were let go, several of them elderly women. The country's president showed up in person. Then, at half past one in the morning, explosions shook the street, and special forces stormed the store. Minutes later, it was over. Every hostage walked out alive. One captor was killed, and the other four were taken away in handcuffs. And here is the twist: the siege ended on the tenth anniversary of Colombia's peace deal, in a city famous for its walled old town and cruise ships. Thirty people went shopping. All thirty came home.
